# Decision report

None of the four routines should ship as-is.

| Routine              | Verdict             | Main reason                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W1 — Hinge & Push Strength | **Ship-with-edits** | Strong base, but cold-morning warm-up is marginal, several toe/shoulder-loading choices are poor defaults, and the final hip-flexion work is redundant.                                                        |
| W2 — Rotation, Lateral & Pull | **Redesign** | Too many plank/rotation stations, inadequate true pulling for a “pull emphasis” routine, questionable Copenhagen placement, and excessive floor transitions.                                                   |
| W3 — Full-Body Strength & Conditioning | **Ship-with-edits** | Best overall coverage, but the full conditioning wave is incorrectly placed before strength, technical active-flexibility work occurs too late, and some stations conflict with the toe/equipment constraints. |
| W4 — Balance & Active Flexibility | **Ship-with-edits** | Its written interval list reaches 45, but the block labels are wrong; cardio is underrepresented, and several stations are redundant.                                                                          |

The highest-risk assumption is that broad rehab coverage equals adequate rehab dosage. W3’s ten-station physio block is broad, but most structures receive only one 40-second set per side. That is not automatically a complete rehabilitation dose.

## 1. Answers to the six open questions

### 1. Strength before or after the W3 cardio-chain wave?

**Strength should come before the full cardio-chain wave.**

W3’s eight cardio-chain stations are not merely an extended warm-up. They include a swing, two lunge-to-knee-drive intervals, an inchworm, shadowboxing, and a bear crawl. Collectively, those create meaningful leg, trunk, shoulder, and grip fatigue. That will reduce the quality of the subsequent RPE 8–9 split squats, RDLs, rows and presses.

The preferred sequence is:

1. Six-minute progressive warm-up.
2. Four minutes of low-fatigue balance and slow kick skill.
3. Strength block.
4. Heavy straight- and bent-knee calf loading.
5. Cardio-chain block.
6. Lower-load hip, psoas, cuff and active-ROM accessories.
7. Cooldown.

One or two easy cardio intervals can precede strength, but the complete eight-minute wave should not. The evidence synthesis specifically requires that tendon loading progress from slow capacity work and that fast work not occur first or cold; it also states that the light dumbbells need high effort for effective strength work. Preserving strength quality therefore takes priority over using eight demanding intervals as a warm-up. `(synthesis.md, lines 11–20 and 34–38)`

This ordering recommendation is a programming inference; the synthesis does not establish a universal rule that strength must always be the first main block.

### 2. Copenhagen adductor with undiagnosed right lateral-hip pain?

**Drop it as the default station.**

A short-lever Copenhagen is not automatically harmful, but it is a poor “safe common denominator” while the right lateral-hip/upper-buttock pain remains undiagnosed. It combines substantial lateral trunk and hip demand with side-lying compression. W2 already includes lateral lunges, so the Copenhagen is not needed to provide adductor exposure.

Replace intervals 19–20 with:

**Standing wall-press hip-abduction isometric, stance leg L/R.** Stand side-on to the wall, press the wall-side knee into the wall, keep the opposite stance pelvis level, and hold two bouts of approximately 15 seconds within the 40-second interval. Use RPE 6–7 and stop if it reproduces lateral-hip pain.

This follows the synthesis recommendation to use bridges, abduction, bird dogs and hinges while avoiding sustained compression and aggressive stretching into symptoms. `(synthesis.md, lines 39–42)`

The Copenhagen can remain in the exercise library as a later progression after the hip has been assessed and ordinary lateral loading is clearly tolerated.

### 3. Weighted Russian twist: keep or substitute?

**Substitute it.**

There is no evidence in the supplied synthesis showing that Russian twists are categorically unsafe for this athlete’s back. The reason to remove it is poorer risk-to-benefit value within this particular routine: W2 already has bilateral wood chops, push-up-to-side-plank rotation, renegade-row anti-rotation and plank pull-throughs. The twist adds more trunk rotation, this time while the spine is flexed, without filling a missing training category.

Use:

**Unilateral front-rack press-out/hold.** Hold one 4–7 kg dumbbell at the chest, use a split or half-kneeling stance, press it slowly away for two seconds and return for two seconds without allowing the trunk to turn. Switch hands at 20 seconds. RPE 7, ribs down, breathe continuously.

A single-dumbbell dead bug with alternating heel taps would also be suitable, but the front-rack press-out fits W2’s standing rotational-chain identity better.

### 4. Are the physio clusters large enough with one or two sessions and habit calf dosing?

**Not by themselves.**

For the Achilles, the combination can work only when the habit work becomes a documented, progressive dosage system:

* Three counted loading days per week.
* Routine calf work counts toward those days.
* Random escalator or waiting-room sets are bonus volume, not automatically a counted dose.
* Hard tendon days are separated by approximately 48–72 hours.
* Pain is normally kept around 0–3/10, never allowed to exceed the stated 5/10 ceiling, and must settle by the next morning without an upward weekly trend. `(synthesis.md, lines 11–20)`

The present workout volume is thin:

* W1: one straight-knee set per side.
* W2: one bent-knee set per side.
* W3: one straight- and one bent-knee set per side.
* W4: one straight-knee set per side.

A 40-second 3-1-3 calf interval produces approximately five or six slow repetitions. That can be a valid hard set, but the synthesis does not establish that one such set is a complete rehabilitation session.

The habit calf work also does nothing for the lateral hip, foot intrinsics, psoas or cuff. With only one guaranteed app session per week, rotating those components between four routines could leave a body region untouched for several weeks. The synthesis itself identifies this weakness and recommends the hybrid “clinic day plus rehab riders” architecture. `(synthesis.md, lines 78–81 and 91–105)`

Therefore:

* W3 and revised W4 should retain straight- and bent-knee calf pairs.
* W1 and W2 can remain lighter riders.
* These four mixed routines should not be treated as the complete physio system.
* A dedicated clinic routine, or a non-negotiable common rehab spine across all routine choices, is still needed.

### 5. Is 40/20 appropriate for near-failure strength, or should key stations use 60/30?

**Keep 40/20 for these routines. Do not change selected stations to 60/30.**

Forty seconds is enough for a productive stable strength set when difficulty is adjusted through leverage, unilateral stance, pauses, range and tempo. With the available light dumbbells, a typical strength prescription should be:

* Three-second eccentric.
* Brief pause at the difficult position.
* Controlled concentric.
* RPE 8 near the end of the interval.
* Occasional RPE 9 only on stable, technically simple stations.

RPE 8–9 should **not** be the global effort target for every interval:

| Station type                                          | Target                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Squats, split squats, RDLs, rows, controlled push-ups | RPE 8; occasional 9                                 |
| Horse-stance or other stable strength holds           | RPE 8                                               |
| Calf loading                                          | RPE about 8, subject to pain and next-morning rules |
| Swings, snatches, shadowboxing                        | RPE 6–7; never technical failure                    |
| Slow kicks and balance                                | RPE 5–7; quality before fatigue                     |
| Cuff, short-foot and low-load hip control             | RPE 5–7                                             |

This preserves the evidence-based requirement that light dumbbell strength sets approach high effort without treating power, balance or prophylactic cuff work as near-failure exercises. `(synthesis.md, lines 34–38)`

Sixty-second sets with these light weights may primarily add local endurance and reduce the exercise variety that defines these routines. A separate strength-focused timer format could exist later, but it is not needed here.

### 6. Does anything break the fun, no-repeat, whole-body or safety brief?

Yes.

1. **“Push-ups on fists if first-MTP complains” is incorrect.** Fists change the hand and wrist position; they do nothing to reduce great-toe extension. Toe-safe regressions are knees-down push-ups, hands-elevated push-ups, or another position in which the forefoot is not loaded. The same issue applies to renegade rows, mountain climbers, bear planks, inchworms and bear crawls. The synthesis specifically says to protect the first MTP from loaded hyperextension. `(synthesis.md, lines 25–28)`

2. **W2 is a plank gauntlet rather than an effective pull/lateral routine.** Renegade row, push-up to side plank, plank pull-through and mountain climber all load the shoulder and forefoot in similar positions. This is likely to feel repetitive despite not being an exact exercise repeat.

3. **W3 overuses hip flexion.** Knee-lift cardio, lunge-to-knee-drive, knee-drive shadowboxing, seated psoas raises, front kicks and long-sitting straight-leg raises collectively create six versions of the same dominant action. That is unnecessarily dense for someone with a prior symptomatic iliopsoas.

4. **W4 lacks an adequate explicit cardio component.** Snatches and suitcase marching raise the heart rate, but the routine is otherwise dominated by balance, static strength and active-flexibility work.

5. **The literal no-repeat rule is broken by the calf primers.** W1, W3 and W4 use calf raises in the warm-up and calf raises again as work stations. This is physiologically unobjectionable but violates the stated product rule. Use ankle pumps and heel-to-toe rocks during the warm-up instead.

6. **W4 repeats the same scapular objective.** Warm-up wall slides, push-up plus and serratus wall slides are excessive within one session. Keep the first two and replace the later serratus station.

7. **The W3 cuff station does not match the equipment.** The lightest available dumbbell is approximately 4–5 kg. That may be much too heavy for prophylactic side-lying external rotation. Use a pain-free wall isometric instead.

8. **No-jumping is respected**, provided swings and snatches are explicitly cued with feet planted and no jump or calf pop. This should be understood as the present capacity-building phase, not permanent abandonment of spring; the evidence proposes a later criteria-gated return continuum. `(synthesis.md, lines 17–20)`

## 2. Arithmetic and cold-morning warm-up

| Routine                 |                                                            Draft arithmetic | Result           |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------: | ---------------- |
| W1                      |                                  5 warm-up + 18 main + 4 rehab + 3 finisher | **30 intervals** |
| W2                      |                                  5 warm-up + 18 main + 4 rehab + 3 finisher | **30 intervals** |
| W3                      |                                                     6 + 8 + 12 + 10 + 6 + 3 | **45 intervals** |
| W4 block labels         |                                                      6 + 6 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 3 | **43, not 45**   |
| W4 actual written items | 7 warm-up + 6 balance + 12 strength + 8 physio + 8 active-flex + 4 cooldown | **45 intervals** |

W4 reaches 45 only when:

* Front-back leg swings L and R are separate warm-up intervals.
* Hip-flexor stretches L and R are separate cooldown intervals.

Therefore the routine’s actual headings must be changed to **Warm-up (7)** and **Cooldown (4)**. Do not leave this to an app parser to infer.

Thirty cycles of 40 seconds plus 20 seconds equal exactly 30:00. Forty-five cycles equal 45:00. This assumes the timer retains the final 20-second recovery. If it stops immediately after the final 40-second work period, the sessions become 29:40 and 44:40.

### Warm-up adequacy

* **W1: five minutes is marginal and should become six.**
* **W2: five minutes is inadequate because it contains no ankle/Achilles preparation and leads directly into loaded squatting and lateral lunging. It should become six.**
* **W3: six minutes is an acceptable minimum**, provided the second station becomes ankle preparation rather than a full calf-loading set.
* **W4: the actual seven-minute warm-up is adequate**, provided the early leg swings are small controlled pendulums rather than ballistic swings.

The synthesis does not establish a precise six-minute threshold. This is a conservative profile-specific judgement based on cold morning training, Achilles symptoms and the plan to reach high effort shortly afterward. The 30-minute routines can gain the extra minute by deleting redundant stations rather than increasing total duration.

A suitable common ramp is:

1. Brisk march.
2. Ankle pumps and heel-to-toe rocks.
3. Gradually deepening squat or lateral shift.
4. Hinge and reach.
5. Scapular wall reach or wall slide.
6. Low-impact lateral step with relaxed arm sweep.

## 3. Interval-by-interval audit

The following constraints apply throughout:

* Achilles loading follows the pain and next-morning rules above. Calf work uses approximately 3 seconds up, a short pause and 3 seconds down, with no bouncing. `(synthesis.md, lines 11–20)`
* Plank-type stations use a knees-down or otherwise toe-unloaded version if the first MTP is symptomatic. `(synthesis.md, lines 25–28)`
* Shoulder work stops for apprehension, instability sensation or persistent pain. Behind-the-neck and loaded end-range abduction/external rotation remain excluded. `(synthesis.md, lines 29–33)`
* Side-lying compression and aggressive hip stretching are not default choices while the right lateral-hip pain is unresolved. `(synthesis.md, lines 39–42)`

This preserves all six required components and removes the original standing knee raise, bear plank and side-plank pair.

## Single most valuable structural improvement

**Separate rehabilitation dosage from workout variety.**

The athlete has only one guaranteed app session each week. Therefore the randomly selected “fun” routine must not determine whether the Achilles, lateral hip, foot, psoas and shoulder receive meaningful exposure. Implement the synthesis’s hybrid principle in concrete form:

* A fixed, countable rehab spine appears in every mixed routine.
* The 45-minute routines add the larger straight-knee/bent-knee calf and accessory clusters.
* A dedicated clinic routine remains available for comprehensive work.
* Habit calf loading is counted as progressive dose days rather than treated as incidental activity.

This is more valuable than adding another clever exercise. It prevents broad-but-shallow physio blocks from being mistaken for adequate rehabilitation and preserves the identities and enjoyment of the four mixed routines. `(synthesis.md, lines 91–105)`
