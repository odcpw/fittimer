# Research: mid-portion Achilles tendinopathy loading

> Provenance: Claude research agent (web search), 2026-08-10. Citations are
> agent-reported with links; spot-verify before clinical reliance. Grades:
> strong / moderate / emerging / expert-opinion.

## Findings

1. JOSPT CPG 2024 (Martin et al., JOSPT 54(12)): first-line care = education plus high-load exercise prescribed at least 3x/week at maximal tolerable intensity, combining isometric and isotonic work and progressing to plyometrics; discourage complete rest, continue activity within pain tolerance. Grade: strong.
2. Alfredson 1998 (Am J Sports Med): 3×15 straight-knee + 3×15 bent-knee eccentric heel drops off a step, twice daily, 7 d/week, 12 weeks; progress by adding backpack load; moderate pain during exercise permitted. Grade: moderate (cohort origin, widely replicated).
3. Beyer/Kongsgaard 2015 RCT (AJSM): HSR — 3 gym sessions/week, three bilateral exercises (standing, seated, leg-press calf raise), 3 s up/3 s down, progressing 3×15RM (wk1) → 3×12RM (wk2–3) → 4×10RM (wk4–5) → 4×8RM (wk6–8) → 4×6RM (wk9–12) — equalled Alfredson at 12 and 52 weeks (VISA-A ~85–89) with higher patient satisfaction (96%). Grade: strong.
4. Habets 2021 RCT (OJSM): Alfredson vs Silbernagel combined loading — both improved markedly (VISA-A 89 vs 83 at 1 yr), no between-group difference. Bottom line: protocol choice is preference/logistics; progressive load is the active ingredient. Grade: moderate (n=40).
5. Silbernagel 2007 RCT (AJSM): continuing sport (running/jumping) under a pain-monitoring model did not impair recovery vs rest. Grade: strong.
6. Pain-monitoring rules (Silbernagel/Thomeé): pain up to 5/10 during/after loading acceptable; must settle by next morning; pain and morning stiffness must not escalate week to week — if they do, drop back a stage. Grade: strong (RCT-embedded, guideline-endorsed).
7. Stevens & Tan 2014 (JOSPT): "do-as-tolerated" eccentric volume was non-inferior to the full 180 reps/day — total daily volume is flexible. Grade: moderate.
8. Mechanistic frequency data: tenocyte anabolic signalling saturates after ~10 min of cyclic loading and needs recovery to restore mechanosensitivity; rest-inserted loading enhances collagen synthesis (BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2014; Sci Rep 2024). Brief bouts separated by hours are mechanistically sound. Grade: emerging.
9. But load magnitude is the decisive stimulus: ~4.5–6.5% tendon strain, ~3 s loading cycles, high intensity drives adaptation (Arampatzis group, Sci Rep 2024; Bohm 2015 review). Unloaded escalator lowers are useful analgesic "snacks" but under-dose the remodelling stimulus needed for return to plyometrics — structured, progressively weighted sessions are meaningfully better; micro-dosing alone has never been trialled as a stand-alone treatment. Grade: moderate/expert-opinion.
10. Isometrics: Rio 2015 analgesia (patellar) failed to replicate in Achilles (O'Neill 2020 quasi-RCT: no immediate relief); meta-analysis (Clifford 2020, BMJ Open SEM) found isometrics not superior to isotonic. Optional, not essential. Grade: moderate.
11. Return-to-sport staging (Silbernagel & Crossley, JOSPT 2015): 4 phases — (1) symptomatic: 2-leg/1-leg raises daily; (2) add eccentrics and load; (3) (~wk 5–12) add fast-rebounding heel raises, then 2-leg hopping/skipping; (4) sport-specific plyometrics. Gates: pain ≤5 during, back to baseline by morning, and roughly ≥20–25 single-leg heel rises (or ≥90% of better side) plus pain ≤3 on trial hops before single-leg plyometrics; expect 3–6 months to full spring, up to 12 for complete recovery. Grade: moderate/expert-opinion.
12. Age 45: tendon retains plasticity but adapts more slowly; heavy loading (≥70% 1RM) works in middle age (GeroScience 2021) — same protocol, longer runway, stricter symptom monitoring. Grade: moderate.
13. Cold mornings: tendons are stiffest on waking; 10–15 min progressive warm-up increases tendon blood flow/compliance (Scand J Med Sci Sports 2021); cold-environment training is a paratendinitis risk factor (Milgrom 2003). Do springy work late in a session, never cold. Grade: emerging/expert-opinion.
14. Stretching: JOSPT 2024 CPG — clinicians "may" use calf stretching if it relieves symptoms; not harmful for MID-portion (unlike insertional, where dorsiflexion compression is avoided). Neutral-to-mildly-helpful adjunct only. Grade: weak/moderate.

## Consensus protocol

HSR 3x/week, 12 weeks, both legs: standing + seated + leg-press (or stair) calf raises, 3 s up/3 s down, 3×15RM → 4×6RM per Beyer schedule; no-gym alternative: weighted-backpack Alfredson 3×15 straight + bent knee, 1–2x/day, volume as tolerated. Keep escalator micro-doses as supplements, not the program. From ~week 4–6, add fast rebound heel raises → 2-leg hopping → skipping → 1-leg hops, gated by ≥20–25 single-leg heel rises, pain ≤3, morning stiffness stable. Pain rule throughout: ≤5/10, settled by next morning, no weekly trend upward. 10–15 min warm-up before morning martial arts; springy drills last, 48 h between hard plyometric days initially.

**Contested:** isometric analgesia for Achilles; any eccentric-specific advantage (likely none — magnitude matters); optimal frequency (daily vs 3x/week equivalent; pure micro-dosing untested); whether stretching adds anything; exact heel-rise RTS thresholds (expert-derived, not validated).
