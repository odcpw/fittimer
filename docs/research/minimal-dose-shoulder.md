# Research: minimal-dose training at 45; shoulder 20+ years post-stabilization

> Provenance: Claude research agent (web search), 2026-08-10. Citations are
> agent-reported with links; spot-verify before clinical reliance. Grades:
> strong / moderate / emerging / expert-opinion.

## Topic A — Minimal-dose training at middle age

1. Iversen et al. 2021 (Sports Med, narrative review): ~4 hard weekly sets/muscle suffice for meaningful strength/hypertrophy; prioritize bilateral multi-joint push/pull/leg movements, use supersets/drop-sets/rest-pause, skip general warm-up and stretching — sessions <30 min are viable. Grade: moderate.
2. Androulakis-Korakakis 2020 (Sports Med): single set of 6–12 reps to ~failure, 2–3×/wk, maintains/improves 1RM in trained men. Grade: moderate.
3. Momma 2022 (BJSM meta, 16 cohorts): 30–60 min/wk of muscle-strengthening → 10–20% lower all-cause/CVD/cancer mortality; J-shaped, no extra benefit past ~1 h/wk. Grade: strong (observational).
4. Weekend-warrior cohorts (O'Donovan 2017 JAMA Int Med; Khurshid 2023 JAMA/Circulation 2024): guideline volume compressed into 1–2 sessions yields mortality/CVD benefits equivalent to spread-out activity. Grade: strong (observational).
5. VILPA (Stamatakis 2022 Nat Med, UK Biobank accelerometry): 3–4 min/day of 1–2-min vigorous incidental bouts → ~26–40% lower all-cause and ~49% lower CVD mortality in non-exercisers — direct support for micro-dosing. Grade: strong (observational).
6. Exercise snacking (Perkin 2019; Islam/Gibala/Little 2022; Sports Med scoping review 2024): ≤1-min bouts spread daily improve leg strength, function and CRF, with high adherence. Grade: emerging.
7. Schoenfeld 2017 (JSCR meta, 21 RCTs): low-load (≤60%1RM) taken to/near failure = equal hypertrophy to heavy loads; heavy remains superior for 1RM strength. Light loads work only if sets are genuinely hard (Lasevicius 2019). Grade: strong.
8. Tempo (Schoenfeld 2015 Sports Med meta): 0.5–8 s reps equivalent for hypertrophy; >10 s inferior. Slow tempo/pauses/1.5-reps don't add growth per rep but usefully bring light loads to failure sooner — a time tool, not a magic stimulus. Grade: strong.
9. Honest limits: 4–7 kg DBs cannot progressively overload hinge/squat strength for a trained 87 kg male; use unilateral loading (split squats, single-leg RDL, step-ups), then the ceiling is real. Tendon adaptation needs high-magnitude (~70–90% MVC) loading; high-rep burn sets are a weak tendon stimulus, but long heavy isometrics substitute (Bohm & Arampatzis 2015 meta). Grade: moderate/expert-opinion.
10. Circuit RT: equal strength/lean-mass vs traditional sets when effort is matched, in ~half the time, plus cardiometabolic benefit (HICT RCTs; Ramos-Campo 2021 meta). Grade: moderate.
11. Isometrics and BP (Edwards 2023 BJSM network meta, 270 RCTs): isometric training best mode for resting BP (−8.2/−4.0 mmHg); wall squat top submode; effective dose ≈ 4×2 min, 3×/wk — planks/wall-sits/horse stance slot into 40/20 intervals. Isometric strength gains are joint-angle-specific with limited dynamic transfer (Oranchuk 2019). Grade: strong (BP) / moderate (strength).
12. Morning training: strength is diurnally ~5–10% lower in the morning, mitigated by an extended specific warm-up; no evidence morning training increases injury; RAMP (Jeffreys) is expert framework — evidence supports ramped dynamic/specific warm-up sets only, no static stretching (Iversen 2021). Grade: moderate/expert-opinion.

## Topic B — Shoulder, 20+ years post open anterior stabilization

13. Long-term open Bankart outcomes: ~10% redislocation at 16–29 y; radiographic OA in ~50–70% but mostly mild (Samilson-Prieto grade 1); small minority progress to arthroplasty (Berendes 2017 Arch Orthop Trauma Surg; Fabre 2006, 29-y). Grade: moderate.
14. Arthropathy tracks with the instability history and recurrence, not with continued strength training; staying strong and avoiding re-subluxation is the protective lever. Grade: expert-opinion.
15. Reinold 2004 (JOSPT EMG): sidelying ER = highest infraspinatus/teres minor activity (62%/67% MVIC) with low deltoid; full-can and prone horizontal abduction at 100° best for supraspinatus. 4–7 kg is an appropriate load range here. Grade: strong (EMG) / moderate (outcomes).
16. Serratus/scapular: push-up plus maximizes serratus (Ludewig 2004 AJSM); wall slides and prone Y/T give high lower-trap with favorable upper-trap ratio (Cools 2007 AJSM). Grade: moderate.
17. Behind-the-neck press/pulldown loads the exact abduction+ER end-range that anterior repairs protect; front variants deliver identical muscle activation with less capsular stress (Sweeney; McKean & Burkett 2015 nuance: safe only with ideal ROM). For this history, permanent avoidance costs nothing and is standard expert advice. Grade: expert-opinion.
18. Return to collision/overhead sport post-stabilization is criteria-based, not time-based; overhead athletes have the lowest return rates (IJSPT scoping review 2023; Popchak, Ann Joint). A back-handspring is ballistic end-range extension under impact; the months-long flare indicates exposure exceeded capacity. Trainable in principle via months of graded end-range isometrics → wall work → spotted drills, but risk:reward at 45 is poor and expert guidance treats it as optional, not advisable. Grade: expert-opinion.
19. Handstands/overhead pressing are stacked closed-chain axial compression, not the anterior-provocation position; tolerated loading maintains capacity and his current comfort is the best available evidence — continue, progress gradually. Grade: expert-opinion.

## Consensus recommendations

**A — Structure:** Guaranteed session: 10-min ramped specific warm-up (morning-adjusted), then ~18+ min of 40/20 circuit: unilateral leg, push, hinge, pull, isometric, core/carry stations; every strength set near-failure — with light DBs, effort is the whole game. Optional second session: overhead press, snatch, handstand work. Daily micro-dosing: 2–4 VILPA-style bouts — independently mortality-relevant. Total ≥30–60 min/wk strengthening hits the Momma sweet spot.

**B — Shoulder micro-routine (3×/wk, ~5 min, habit-anchored):** sidelying ER 2×12–15 @ 2–4 kg; push-up plus 2×10; wall slides or prone Y 2×10; one 30-s end-range-shy overhead isometric hold. **Verdict:** keep avoiding behind-the-neck and loaded end-range abduction+ER permanently — zero benefit forgone; keep handstands and overhead pressing. Back handsprings: not recommended at 45 with this history; if non-negotiable, 3–6 months graded end-range prep plus spotted progression, accepting elevated re-flare risk.
