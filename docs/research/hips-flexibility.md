# Research: lateral hip pain, hip-flexor strength through range, active flexibility

> Provenance: Claude research agent (web search), 2026-08-10. Citations are
> agent-reported with links; spot-verify before clinical reliance. Grades:
> strong / moderate / emerging / expert-opinion.

## Topic A — Lateral hip/upper-buttock pain with sitting

1. LEAP RCT (Mellor et al., BMJ 2018, n=204): education + progressive abductor loading beat corticosteroid injection and wait-and-see for gluteal tendinopathy — 77% vs 58% vs 29% "moderately better" at 8 weeks, advantage maintained at 52 weeks. Grade: strong (single large high-quality RCT; cost-effectiveness confirmed in 2022 economic analysis).
2. Grimaldi & Fearon (JOSPT 2015): gluteal tendinopathy is compression-driven; management centers on reducing sustained hip adduction (leg-crossing, hanging on one hip, side-lying on affected side without pillow) plus low-velocity, high-load abductor exercise (isometric wall-press abduction, bridging, hip hitch); avoid aggressive ITB/adductor stretching early. Grade: moderate (clinical commentary underpinning the LEAP protocol).
3. Deep gluteal syndrome (Martin et al., Knee Surg Sports Traumatol Arthrosc 2015) and proximal hamstring tendinopathy (Goom et al., JOSPT 2016) both classically provoke pain with prolonged sitting/driving; PHT localizes to the ischial tuberosity, DGS more diffuse buttock ± posterior-thigh symptoms. Both respond to graded loading and avoiding sustained compressive/stretch positions — congruent with the LEAP approach. Grade: moderate (PHT loading), emerging (DGS conservative care).
4. SIJ pain and lumbar referral are ruled in/out clinically (provocation-test clusters, Laslett 2005; centralization with repeated lumbar movements). Isometric/bridging abductor work is not contraindicated in either, so the shared-safe exercise set holds across all differentials. Grade: moderate.
5. Red flags requiring assessment: night pain unrelieved by position change, progressive neurological deficit, saddle anaesthesia/bladder change, fever, unexplained weight loss, history of cancer or significant trauma, or no improvement after 6–8 weeks of graded loading. Grade: expert-opinion/guideline consensus.

## Topic B — Hip-flexor strengthening through range (>90°)

6. EMG work (Andersson et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports 1995/1997; confirmed in recent exercise-EMG reviews) shows iliopsoas is the dominant — effectively the only meaningful — hip flexor above ~90°; rectus femoris/TFL/sartorius contribution falls off. Above-90° training must therefore be position-specific. Grade: moderate.
7. Deane et al. (J Strength Cond Res 2005, RCT): 8 weeks of resisted hip-flexor training improved sprint and shuttle-run times in healthy adults — direct evidence that hip-flexor strengthening transfers to performance. Grade: moderate (small RCT).
8. Copenhagen group (Thorborg, Hölmich, Ishøi): hip-flexion strength/rate-of-force deficits accompany hip-related groin pain and FAI (Freke et al., BJSM 2016 systematic review), and progressive strengthening is first-line in groin-pain consensus (Doha agreement, Weir et al., BJSM 2015). No RCT yet isolates above-90° iliopsoas training for kicking; kicking-velocity association is plausible but indirect. Grade: emerging.
9. His prior success with seated active knee-lift + slow eccentric lowering matches tendinopathy-style loading principles (heavy-slow resistance, Beyer et al. 2015) applied to iliopsoas. Grade: expert-opinion extension of moderate tendon evidence.

## Topic C — Active flexibility / strength at long lengths

10. Afonso et al. (Healthcare 2021, meta-analysis, 11 RCTs): strength training through full ROM improves flexibility as much as stretching; supported by Alizadeh et al. 2023 meta (resistance training alone increases ROM). Grade: strong-to-moderate.
11. Warneke et al. (Eur J Appl Physiol 2023 and related trials): very long-duration daily stretching (≥15 min–2 h) produces hypertrophy, strength and ROM gains comparable to resistance training — evidence that loaded long-length work and stretching converge mechanistically. Grade: moderate (calf-specific, young subjects).
12. PAILs/RAILs end-range isometrics: no direct RCTs; branding around plausible mechanisms (isometrics at long muscle length, eccentric-induced fascicle lengthening — O'Sullivan et al., BJSM 2012). Useful, not uniquely effective. Grade: expert-opinion.
13. Thomas et al. (Int J Sports Med 2018, review): weekly time-under-stretch is the key dose variable — ≥5 min/muscle/week, spread over ≥5 days, static holds superior long-term to ballistic/PNF. Grade: moderate. Evening timing: no convincing chronic-adaptation evidence; train when compliant. Grade: expert-opinion.
14. Slow-motion kick work: no direct trials; specificity of motor learning and balance-training evidence (tai-chi-style slow control) support it for active end-range control. Grade: expert-opinion.
15. Asymmetry: stretching/strength adaptations are side- and joint-specific, so extra dosing on the stiff side is the defensible approach; no evidence that forced symmetry per se reduces injury. Grade: expert-opinion.
    (Note: oracle's report tempers this — asymmetry cutoffs are not diagnostic; default to symmetric dosing, re-test monthly, add one extra set to a consistently restricted side for 6–8 weeks. Adopted in synthesis.)

## Consensus recommendations

**A:** 3×/week: wall-press abduction isometric (40s work/20s rest ×3/side, ~5/10 effort max), side-lying or standing hip hitch (40/20 ×3), glute bridge → single-leg bridge (40/20 ×3). Daily: avoid leg-crossing, sitting on wallet, prolonged driving without breaks; pillow between knees side-lying. Expect 8+ weeks; refer out if red flags or no change by week 6–8.

**B:** 2–3×/week: seated knee-lift above 90° with 3–4s eccentric (40/20 ×3/side, add light load when easy), standing wall-supported knee raise to max height with 2s end-range hold (40/20 ×3), progress to L-sit tuck holds (accumulate 20–30s). Keep the pattern that already resolved his psoas pain as the base exercise.

**C:** After sessions, 3–5×/week: loaded long-length work (rear-foot-elevated hip-flexor "stretch" with 4–7 kg goblet hold, 40/20 ×3/side) + static holds totaling ≥5 min/week per target (60s holds); end-range lift-offs (leg raised to max, lift 5° higher, hold 10–20s) for active ROM; slow-motion kicks 2×/week (40/20 ×4). Stiff side: modest extra dosing, re-test monthly (see note in finding 15).
