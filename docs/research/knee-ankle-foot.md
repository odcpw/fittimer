# Research: knee/ankle resilience (knees-over-toes claims), isometrics, foot care

> Provenance: Claude research agent (web search), 2026-08-10. Citations are
> agent-reported with links; spot-verify before clinical reliance. Grades:
> strong / moderate / emerging / expert-opinion / hype.

## Topic A — ATG/knees-over-toes methods

1. Restricting forward knee travel reduced knee torque ~22% but increased hip/low-back torque ~1000% (Fry et al. 2003, J Strength Cond Res). Knees-past-toes squatting is not inherently dangerous; the "knees behind toes" rule is myth. **Strong.**
2. Deep squats with progressive load show no greater injury or degeneration risk than partial squats and may enhance knee stability (Hartmann et al. 2013, Sports Medicine review). **Strong.**
3. Knee-loading exercise is not harmful to cartilage in at-risk knees; some markers suggest positive adaptation (Bricca et al. 2019, BJSM systematic review). Caveat: lifetime occupational squatting/kneeling epidemiologically associates with knee OA (Zhang et al. 2004) — recreational dose appears fine. **Moderate.**
4. VMO cannot be selectively activated (Smith et al. 2009, systematic review); general quadriceps strengthening improves patellofemoral pain (van der Heijden et al. 2015, Cochrane). "VMO isolation"/terminal-knee-extension specificity claims: **hype**; quad strengthening itself: **strong**.
5. Tibialis anterior raises: no RCTs for injury prevention; medial tibial stress syndrome prevention evidence is inconclusive (Winters et al. 2013, Sports Med). Cheap and low-risk. **Plausible-but-unproven.**
6. Retro-walking: 6-week RCT showed backward walking superior to forward walking for pain, function, and quadriceps strength in knee OA (Alghadir et al. 2019, BMC Musculoskelet Disord); replicated for balance/pain (Chen et al. 2021); mechanistically lowers patellofemoral compressive stress (Flynn & Soutas-Little 1995). **Moderate.**
7. Nordic hamstring programs cut hamstring injuries ~50% across 8,459 athletes (van Dyk et al. 2019, BJSM meta-analysis). **Strong** — for hamstrings; knee benefit is indirect.
8. ATG split squat, Petersen step-up, backward sled as branded methods: no direct trials (**expert-opinion**), but consistent with supported principles: loaded deep knee flexion (finding 2) and heavy slow resistance for tendon (Malliaras et al. 2013).

## Topic B — Deep stances and isometrics

9. Isometric training is joint-angle specific; long-muscle-length (deep) holds transfer best to dynamic strength and drive tendon adaptation (Oranchuk et al. 2019, Scand J Med Sci Sports review). **Moderate.**
10. Acute isometric analgesia in patellar tendinopathy (Rio et al. 2015) has not consistently replicated (Clifford et al. 2020). **Emerging.**
11. Isometric training is the most effective exercise modality for lowering resting BP (−8.2/−4.0 mmHg; wall squat top submode) across 270 RCTs (Edwards et al. 2023, BJSM network meta-analysis). **Strong.** Caution: acute pressor response during long holds is substantial at 45 — breathe continuously, no Valsalva; check with a physician if hypertensive.
12. Horse-stance holds specifically: untrialed (**expert-opinion**) but well supported indirectly by 9 and 11.

## Topic C — Foot

13. Short-foot/intrinsic training improves toe-flexor strength, navicular drop, and balance in small RCTs; "foot core" framework (McKeon et al. 2015, BJSM). **Moderate** for strength/balance; injury prevention **expert-opinion**.
14. Toe-flexor weakness independently predicts falls in older adults (Mickle et al. 2009, Clin Biomech); progressive toe-flexor resistance restores strength (Mickle et al. 2016). **Moderate.**
15. Hallux valgus: toe-spread-out exercise reduced HV angle and increased abductor hallucis size (Kim et al. 2015, small RCT); mobilization + exercise + toe separator improved 1-year outcomes (RCT 2018); overall non-surgical evidence for halting progression is limited — symptoms improve more reliably than angle (Hurn et al. 2022, Arthritis Care Res). **Emerging** (angle), **moderate** (symptoms).
16. Barefoot/minimalist walking grows intrinsic foot muscles as effectively as exercise programs (Ridge et al. 2019, MSSE). Barefoot mat training is compatible with his foot. **Moderate.**
17. With prior sesamoiditis, limit loaded first-MTP hyperextension (rear foot in deep lunges, toes-extended push-ups): **expert-opinion** — use instep-flat rear foot, fists-or-knuckle push-ups, or pad proximal to the sesamoids.
18. Toe cramps on pointing are benign; best evidence favors neuromuscular fatigue over dehydration (Schwellnus 2009, BJSM). Graded pointing exposure. **Expert-opinion.**

## Consensus recommendations

**A (2–3x/wk, 40s/20s circuits):** tibialis raises off step ×3; DB step-ups (slow, full control) ×3/side; deep split squat, bodyweight→DB, rear instep flat on mat (no toe extension) ×3/side; backward walking 5–10 min; Nordic lowerings anchored under furniture, 3×4–6 slow eccentrics, 2x/wk. No jumping; all mat/DB/step compatible.

**B:** timed deep holds at the exact stance depths he uses — horse stance and wall-squat 3–4 × 45s–2min (Edwards-style: 4×2min, 2min rest, 3x/wk for BP benefit); breathe throughout; progress duration before depth.

**C (daily, 40s/20s):** short-foot ×3/foot; toe-spread-out/big-toe abduction ×3; gentle pointing/doming to fatigue-tolerance for cramps; evening toe spacers optional. Cautions: keep first-MTP loading pain-free, avoid deep-lunge rear-toe extension and hard barefoot forefoot impacts; monitor sesamoid area next-day soreness.
